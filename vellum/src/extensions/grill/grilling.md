# Grilling the reviewer

Interview the reviewer until you reach a shared understanding. Map this as a **design tree**: every decision branches into the decisions that hang off it.

Work the tree in **rounds**. The **frontier** is every decision whose prerequisites are already settled: the questions you can ask _now_ without guessing at answers you haven't heard yet. Ask the whole frontier in one round: call `mcp__vellum__grill_ask` once with every question and your recommended answer. The call waits while the reviewer answers in the review page, never in the terminal, and returns their reply, which starts with `Reviewer:`; go on from it in the same turn. If your turn ended first, the reply arrives instead in the "Grill" section of a batch the reviewer sent, "Reviewer sent: read <file>.". What they wrote beside the questions comes first: it is addressed to you, so answer it. Then one `Qn: ...` line per answer they gave, answers only, since the questions are already in your context. A last line may name a batch to read: the comments the reviewer sent with the reply. A line of theirs that starts like an answer comes with a backslash, `\Qn: ...`: it belongs to the text above it, and answers nothing. `Qn: As recommended.` takes your recommendation as you wrote it. A question of the round that is absent from the reply took your recommendation by default: it stands, so go on with it, never ask it again, and record it in the plan as taken by default. `Reviewer: all open questions as recommended.` is a round where every question did. A question of a round the reviewer has not sent yet stays open across anything else that happens in the session; when the grill ends, each one takes your recommendation by default.

Write a recommendation in your own voice: what you would choose and why ("I recommend the local server: ..."). Never write it as the reviewer's answer ("No, leave it.", "If you say yes..."): the reviewer speaks for themselves, in their field.

Each round the reviewer answers reshapes the tree: settled decisions push the frontier outward and unblock questions that depended on them. Recompute the frontier and ask the next round. A question whose answer depends on another question still open in this round belongs to a _later_ round, not this one.

Finding _facts_ is your job, never the reviewer's. When a frontier question needs a fact from the environment (filesystem, tools, etc.), dispatch a sub-agent to find it; don't ask the reviewer for anything you could look up yourself. Don't block on it: a running exploration is an unsettled prerequisite, so only the questions downstream of it wait for the sub-agent to report; ask the rest of the frontier now. The _decisions_ are the reviewer's: put each to them and wait.

A question is asked only when the answer would change the architecture, an interface or the scope. Anything else: take the recommended option and record it in the plan under Assumptions.

You never end a grill. When the frontier is empty, every branch of the design tree visited and nothing left silently assumed, say so in one line and wait: the reviewer ends it from the page. A prompt then says the grill ended. Every answer is already in your context: do not reread the transcript. Propose the next step with `mcp__vellum__propose`, the plan when nothing is left open, and record each choice still open as an assumption or as a question left open. The transcript is a file of the working directory, kept word for word; name it in the plan under its artifacts.
